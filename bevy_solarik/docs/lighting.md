# Lighting notes

These are the implementation details behind the changes from `bevy_solari` 0.19.1. See the [README](../README.md) for setup.

**A sky.** Upstream lights a scene from directional lights and emissive meshes only; a ray that leaves the scene contributes nothing, so anything lit by the sky alone renders black. Here a `SolarikSkyLight` resource (a cubemap in the scene's radiance units, plus an intensity) is bound into the ray-traced scene and read wherever a traced ray escapes: the ReSTIR GI first bounce (the escaped ray becomes a far sample point and is reused like any other), the world cache's GI rays (traced to the end of the world so distant geometry is not mistaken for sky), the specular GI paths and the pathtracer.

The sky is not in the direct-light list on purpose. A first version put it there as uniform-sphere light samples; in a street canyon almost every sample that wins the resampling is occluded, and the spiky estimate lost about two stops through the tonemapper and the denoiser. Rays that escape are visible by construction, so the miss paths are the better estimator. The first realtime GI bounce now mixes cubemap importance sampling with a cosine hemisphere; both traced hits and misses use the reciprocal mixture PDF. Below-surface sky draws remain counted zero samples. See [sky sampling measurements](sky-sampling.md) for the two-pass CDF, GPU validation and the limits of the measured result.

**Alpha-masked and blended materials.** Upstream builds every BLAS opaque and the rays see leaf cards and window glass as solid quads. Here the binder looks at the materials on each mesh: a mesh with a `Mask` or `Blend` material gets its BLAS rebuilt without the `OPAQUE` flag, and `trace_ray` walks the candidate hits (`rayQueryProceed` / `rayQueryConfirmIntersection`), alpha-testing `Mask` candidates against the base colour texture and never confirming `Blend` ones (glass is transparent to light). Double-sided materials hit from behind are shaded from the side the ray arrived on, as the rasteriser already does. The material flags live in what used to be `GpuMaterial`'s padding, so the layout is unchanged. `SolarikAlphaTesting(false)` turns it all off for a scene that cannot afford the any-hit work (a dense tree in view adds about 2 ms to the lighting passes at 1080p and at 4K). No leaf transmission yet: a leaf lit from the other side stays dark. The reference pathtracer now uses a separate `trace_glass_ray` entry point for camera and BSDF rays, resolving blended hits as smooth thin glass with Fresnel reflection and tinted transmission. Realtime glossy paths also use `trace_glass_ray`, retaining three opaque bounces with a separate 32-pane chain limit. Shadow and diffuse GI rays keep `trace_ray`. See [the glass model and validation](glass.md), including the limits of unattenuated shadow rays.

**Point and spot lights.** Upstream ignores `PointLight` and `SpotLight`; a lamp-lit scene had to fake its lamps as emissive meshes, and the numbers never matched the raster path. Here every point and spot light in the scene is a light source in the ReSTIR DI list, sampled as a sphere of the light's `radius` (floored at 1 cm, since glTF and Bevy default to 0). The units are the raster path's: `bevy_pbr` already turns lumens into candela with its 4π (for spots too), the sphere's surface radiance is that intensity over π r², so a receiver facing the light sees I / d² either way, and Filament's spot cone and range window are applied exactly as `pbr_lighting.wgsl` does. A light with a `SolarikLightCone` component (inner and outer angles, radians from the transform's forward) takes that cone in place of a spot light's, and its outer angle may pass the horizon, up to π: `bevy_pbr` holds a `SpotLight`'s cone under a quarter turn, and a lamp that must light the walls down to the floor without burning the fitting it sits on needs a little more than a hemisphere. On a `PointLight` the intensity inside the cone is the point light's own; the selection flux is the cone's solid angle, and the shaders are the spot light's unchanged. Shadow rays go to the sample point; the sample is a point on the whole sphere (not the cap a receiver would see), because Solari resolves light samples before it knows the receiver (the presampled tiles, and the resampling re-resolves them at other pixels); the far half of the sphere has zero weight and falls out of the resampling without a ray. The sphere is an additive virtual emitter. Ordinary next-event estimation keeps full weight; delta reflection paths and the realtime smooth primary lobe excluded from ReSTIR DI collect ray/sphere hits instead. Directional disks use the same ownership partition. See [analytic reflections](glass.md#analytic-light-reflections). Temporal reuse follows a light across frames by its entity, like a directional light. Diffuse BRDFs differ by design: Solari's Lambert carries a (1 - F) layering term the raster Burley diffuse does not, which is about 0.1 EV on a rough dielectric. The separate local test rig measures this against the pathtracer and a closed-form scene.

**Receivers inside a light's sphere (14 November 2026).** Every point of a light's sphere faces outward, so a receiver inside the sphere had `cos_theta_light` 0 for every sample and got no light from it, while a receiver just outside was at its brightest: a crisp dark disc wherever a light's source sphere (0.25 m on Halo's lights) cut a surface, seen in play on a10's shield tester, a needle stuck in a wall and a grenade lying on the floor. Inside the sphere (`|v|² + 2 r v·n < 0`, with `v` from the sample point to the receiver and `n` the sample's outward normal) the geometry term `cos / d²` is now `1 / (2 r d)` (`calculate_resolved_light_contribution` in `sampling.wesl`). Seen from within, the shell's radiance along a direction is weighted by the share of the chord in front of the receiver, `t+ / (t+ + t−)`, and the inner cosine of a chord is `(t+ + t−) / 2r`, so the uniform point's `cos / d²` times that weight is `1 / (2 r d)`: the irradiance is continuous across the shell for every orientation of the receiver, half the shell's peak at the centre, and constant across a plane through the centre. Receivers outside the sphere are exactly as before; the cone and range window apply as they did. The sample carries the sphere's radius for this (`source_radius`, also in the presampled tiles, whose packed sample grew from 36 to 40 bytes). `SOLARIK_SHADER_DEFS=LIGHT_INSIDE_DARK` is the old.

**Hollow lights (15 November 2026).** An emitter drawn as a model can sit inside its own light's sphere: Halo's Guilty Spark hangs in the middle of a light whose source sphere is 0.25 m, wider than he is. Before the inside term he got nothing from it and his own emissive eye and the shell's dark bands showed; with it his shell is lit from all round at the sphere's interior irradiance, a white ball at any power that still lights a room. `SolarikHollowLight` on a point or spot light's entity marks it hollow: `LocalLight.hollow` (the field that was the struct's padding, so the layout is as it was; `GpuLocalLight::new` takes it beside `unreflected`) makes the resolved sample's `source_radius` 0 for that light in both places a local light is resolved (`sampling.wesl`: the sampled path and `analytic_light_radiance`), so the inside term never applies to it and a receiver inside its sphere gets nothing, as before 14 November, while every receiver outside is exactly as it is for any light. Nothing else reads the flag: the sampling density, the tiles, the history's weight scale and the near-field rule use the light's radius as they did. A hollow light does show the dark disc where its sphere cuts a surface, so it is for emitters that float clear of the level, not for lamps. Unit test `a_hollow_light_differs_only_by_its_flag` (`binder.rs`). `tests/analytic_lights_fixture.wgsl` keeps its own older `LocalLight` and does not know the flag.

**A resized light's history (14 November 2026).** A ReSTIR DI reservoir holds a light sample (the light's index and a seed) and an unbiased contribution weight, an inverse density over the light's area. The next frame finds the light again through `previous_frame_light_id_translations` and resolves the sample from its seed on the light as it is then. When a point or spot light's sphere changed radius from `r_old` to `r`, the point moved under a uniform scaling whose area Jacobian is `(r / r_old)²`, but the weight was carried over as it was. The sphere's radiance is its intensity over `π r²`, so a light that shrank read `(r_old / r)²` too bright through every temporal reservoir holding it, and one that grew read too dim, until the history turned over (the confidence cap of 8 makes that a decay of 8/9 a frame). Halo's bridge lightning on b40 and c40 shows this: each cluster of bolt sprites is a sphere light whose radius is the cluster's spread, and a light that went from 0.87 m to 0.1 m read 75 times too bright, which flashed the whole bridge several times a second. Each translation now carries a `weight_scale` beside the light's new index, `(r / r_old)²` for a sphere light present in both frames (the radius the GPU uses, at least `LOCAL_LIGHT_MIN_RADIUS`) and 1 for everything else, and `load_temporal_reservoir` multiplies the weight by it (`light_history_weight_scale` in `binder.rs`). A reused frame keeps the uploaded translations, so a frame whose scales were not all 1 settles the light history and the next frame rebuilds them, as it does when the lights change. Only ReSTIR DI's reservoirs carry a light sample's weight from frame to frame: the world cache's light slots are proposal weights evaluated by the same probability they draw by, and ReSTIR GI's reservoirs hold sample points, not light samples. `SOLARIK_SHADER_DEFS=LIGHT_HISTORY_UNSCALED` is the old.

**Sharp surfaces' reflections end in the world cache (14 November 2026).** The specular pass's glossy path ended in the world cache only at its third hit or where it was long and the path already rough, so a near-mirror surface's reflection took one random light sample among the level's lights at its first hit and bounced on. Master Chief's visor (an opaque metal of roughness 0.0144) was black with about one lit speck in fifty pixels, where a sample found a light, and DLSS smeared the specks into a flat, dull colour in a well-lit room. A surface whose roughness (alpha) is under `SPECULAR_SHARP_ROUGHNESS` now has its own path end like a camera pane's reflection (see [what a pane's reflection reads](glass.md#what-a-panes-reflection-reads-grime-and-bump)): at its first opaque hit that is not a mirror, the hit's emission plus its base colour times the world cache's spread irradiance there, without that surface's glossy highlights. A perfect mirror counts too, and its reflection now also ends at its first non-mirror hit. A clear coat's lobe is left out by default: every coat in Halo is under the knob (Chief's and the Elites' armour, the eyes, the Hunters' worms, the Covenant vehicles), and with the rule Chief's armour turned from matte green to a chrome-like mirror in a lit room, a look change nobody asked for; `SOLARIK_SPECULAR_SHARP_COATS=1` gives the coats the rule as well. With `NRC_SPECULAR` the main lobe's path asks NRC there as before. `SOLARIK_SPECULAR_SHARP_ROUGHNESS` is the knob in thousandths of alpha (25, about perceptual roughness 0.16); 0 is the old.

**Pathtracer accumulation.** Upstream resets the accumulation whenever `Ref<GlobalTransform>::is_changed()` says the camera moved, and read it from the extract system that fires every frame for a camera that has not moved, so the reference never got past one sample. This fork compares the transform by value. Two diagnostics came with the fix: the pathtracer node logs (at debug level) how many of the last 100 frames reset the accumulation, and `SOLARIK_PATHTRACER_DEBUG_SAMPLE_COUNT` in the environment at startup makes it draw the per-pixel sample count instead of the image as little-endian RGB bytes (`R + 256*G + 65536*B`, capped at 16,777,215). Use an sRGB PNG target with exposure/tonemapping and other postprocessing disabled (`SCENE_LINEAR=1` in the rig). See [the convergence audit](pathtracer-convergence.md).

**Pathtracer repairs (14 November 2026).** The reference had stopped working inside an application that uses the realtime passes' light tables, and three faults stood between it and a usable reference. Its pipeline reached `light_triangles` (the realtime passes' group 1, binding 30), which its own layout does not have, so the pipeline failed and the view stayed black: the pathtracer's pipeline now sets the def `PATHTRACER`, under which the shared modules leave that table and its users out. A reset requested in the main world (`Pathtracer::reset`, set on a camera cut or a scene change) was never cleared, so from the first one on the accumulation restarted every frame: the extract system clears it once it is read. A light-only emitter, which no ray can hit, shared its next-event weight by MIS with BSDF hits that never come and lost that share: the pipeline sets `UNSEEN_LIGHT_NEE` as the realtime passes do. Russian roulette divided by the throughput's luminance even above 1, where every path survives, which darkened long bright paths: the survival chance is `min(luminance, 1)`. `tests/pathtracer_layout.rs` checks with naga that every global the `pathtrace` entry point uses is in the pipeline's layout. Point lights are sampled as a uniform point on their sphere, so a surface touching a light's sphere has unbounded variance, and a light seen through a small opening needs tens of thousands of frames; compare region means in linear light, since tone mapping clips the fireflies that carry the mean.

**Power-weighted light selection.** Each frame the CPU builds a Walker alias table over estimated emitted flux. Emissive meshes use the material's luminance times world-space triangle area times π; point lights use candela times 4π; spots integrate Bevy's squared cone ramp. Directional lights use illuminance times a nominal 1 m² collection area. Emissive textures are not averaged into the estimate. These choices affect variance; each sample is divided by its actual selection probability, and emissive-hit MIS reads that same probability from the instance. The sky remains on the traced miss paths. Mesh edges are cached at upload (24 bytes per triangle) so area follows nonuniform scale and mesh edits; a dark light list falls back to uniform selection.

On the Bistro at frame 140, the 512-frame pathtracer capture is within 0.03 EV of the uniform 8192-frame reference on two cobblestone patches. Against a weighted 8192-frame reference, patch RMSE drops about 36% from uniform selection at the same warmup. Uniform selection already passed the 0.2 EV mean threshold, and the raw realtime output did not measurably improve. These are single-run measurements, not a 16× convergence claim; see [the measurement record](light-sampling.md).

**Guided first rays for ReSTIR GI.** A diffuse bounce ray's direction comes from the sky mixture (cosine-weighted, plus the sky's bright directions). When a surface's bounce light arrives through a small opening, such as a fire-lit corridor seen through a doorway that fills about 1% of the hemisphere, few pixels find it in a frame, each find carries a weight near a hundred, and the reuse passes spread the finds as slow blotches. With `GI_GUIDE` (on by default; `SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE=0` is the old sampling) a share `q` of the initial candidates is drawn uniformly in a cone about the direction from the pixel to the sample its temporal reservoir already holds. The density is the one-sample mixture `(1 - q) * sky_mixture_pdf + q * cone_pdf`, evaluated at the drawn direction whichever technique drew it (`sample_sky_mixture_guided` in `sky_sampling.wesl`), so the estimate stays unbiased, every direction the old sampler reached keeps at least `1 - q` of its density, and no weight grows by more than `1 / (1 - q)`.

The share is `q = min(GI_GUIDE_SHARE, 0.9) * saturate(1 - W / GI_GUIDE_WIDE)` (`make_gi_guide` in `restir_gi.wesl`), where `W` is the temporal reservoir's unbiased contribution weight: roughly the solid angle the pixel's bounce light would fill if all of it were as bright as the held sample. A pixel lit from a wide part of its hemisphere has a large `W` and samples as before; one lit through a doorway has a small `W` and aims up to half its rays back at the opening. The guide is built from last frame's reservoir before this frame's candidate is drawn. There is no guide when `q` is under 0.01, when the reservoir is empty or holds a non-finite value, or when the held direction is within about 3 degrees of the surface's horizon. With `q = 0` no extra random numbers are drawn, so unguided pixels keep their sequence.

Defaults: share 50% (`SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE_SHARE`, percent), cone 10 degrees (`SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE_CONE`, clamped to 1 to 80), width 1 sr (`SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE_WIDE`, hundredths of a steradian). `SOLARIK_SHADER_DEFS=VISUALIZE_GI_GUIDE` shows red where the candidate came from the cone, the share in green and `W` against the width in blue; view it without DLSS.

Measured in Halo's The Maw at the doorway case, as the standard deviation over time of a still view (of 255, 17 captures 18 frames apart): 0.66 to 0.79 over twelve runs with the guide against 0.86 to 1.01 over seven without, the worst wall blocks 1.6 to 2.5 without and 0.8 to 1.4 with in four runs of five, the mean brightness unchanged (37.5 against 37.7). The diffuse GI pass costs 0.05 to 0.1 ms more; views without such openings measure the same noise with it on and off. Cone 5 to 20 degrees, share 50 to 75% and width 0.5 to 3 sr all measured alike; a width under 0.5 sr varied from run to run. The same guide applied to the world cache's own bounce rays was built and removed: it made no difference to the final picture, because a cell's long average already smooths what pixels read from it.

**Guides from neighbours' histories.** The guide above exists only while the pixel's own temporal reservoir holds a sample of the opening. A pixel whose reservoir holds one of the room's broad samples has `q = 0` and finds the opening by the sky mixture alone, about one ray in a hundred at a weight near a hundred, and the spatial reuse hands that spike to the pixels round it. With `GI_GUIDE_NEIGHBOURS` (8 by default; `SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE_NEIGHBOURS=0` is the guide of the pixel's own history alone, at most 16) a pixel whose own guide has less than nine tenths of the full share reads up to that many of last frame's reservoirs at random places within `GI_GUIDE_RADIUS` pixels (30; `SOLARIK_GI_GUIDE_RADIUS`) of its reprojected place, makes a guide from each toward its sample point as seen from this pixel, and keeps the one with the greatest share. A neighbour is read alone (`load_guide_neighbour_reservoir`): no previous depth or G-buffer and no similarity test, because a guide is only a direction to aim at and `make_gi_guide` already refuses one under the pixel's horizon or not finite. Nothing of a neighbour is merged. The guide is fixed before the candidate is drawn and the candidate is weighted by the mixture of whichever guide stands, so the estimate is unbiased as before and the support is unchanged. With 0 neighbours no extra random number is drawn.

Measured at the same doorway (linear captures under DLSS Ray Reconstruction, 65 captures 18 frames apart, the wall's standard deviation over time in 32-pixel blocks, of 255 at a mean of 17.7): 0.57 and 0.58 without neighbours, 0.26 and 0.30 with 4, 0.17 and 0.21 with 8, 0.16 and 0.20 with 16, the wall's mean unchanged (7.91 to 8.03 without, 7.88 to 8.10 with, in thousandths of full scale) and the same room without the fire's light unchanged in mean and deviation. In the game at the player's view: the deviation of the wall over time in 32-pixel blocks 0.56 to 0.91 of 255 without and 0.22 to 0.49 with, over six runs a side in game at a mean of 36 (0.57 to 0.17 in linear captures in the viewer), the wall as bright. The diffuse GI pass there costs 1.25 ms without, 1.41 with 4, 1.48 with 8 and 1.58 with 16. At four other views in Halo (a canyon, two corridors and a hall) the pass costs 0.17 to 0.25 ms more and the noise and stability measures are unchanged. Against a path-traced reference in linear light the doorway's bounce is about 60% of the reference's with or without the neighbours' guide: the guide steadies the estimate and does not change its mean. Storing the history before the spatial merge was steadier still and wrong: it lost three quarters of the bounce under DLSS, because unshared finds stay rare spikes that the denoiser discards. `SOLARIK_GI_SPATIAL_COUNT` (1) merges more spatial neighbours a frame: at 4 about a third less deviation for 0.27 ms, and no gain beside the neighbours' guide.

**Ray offsets far from the origin.** The realtime passes started rays a fixed `RAY_T_MIN` (1 mm) off a surface and stopped shadow rays 1 mm short of their target. A few kilometres from the origin that is inside single precision's error (one ulp is a quarter of a millimetre at 4000 m, and a hit position is the sum of several products), so bounce, shadow and cache rays met the surface they left and regions went dark, different ones each frame: a ship 2 km out looked like two ships z-fighting. `ray_epsilon(position)` (`raytracing_scene_bindings.wesl`) is the position's greatest coordinate times `RAY_OFFSET_SCALE`, never under `RAY_T_MIN`: at the default scale of 2^-20 exactly 1 mm within 1048 m of the origin. It serves positions known to float precision: a ray's hit, a light's point, a world-cache cell. `surface_ray_epsilon` serves a position reconstructed from the G-buffer's depth and counts the distance from the camera and the raster's snapping too (`pixel_ray_offset` in `gbuffer_utils.wesl`), as ReSTIR DI's `offset_surface_ray` already did. ReSTIR GI, the glossy and glass paths, light sampling, the world cache's update and NRC's training rays use them. `SOLARIK_SHADER_DEFS=RAY_OFFSET_FIXED` restores the fixed millimetre; `SOLARIK_RAY_OFFSET_SCALE` sets the scale in units of 2^-24 (16). The pathtracer uses 2^-20 without a switch. Measured in Halo's The Maw, whose opening shot is 2 to 4 km from the origin: the mean frame-to-frame change in a box on the ship fell from 0.76 to 0.33 of 255, the rest being the camera's motion; four views near the origin measured the same noise and pass times.

**Emitter MIS measure (development).** Reference, glossy and primary-surface paths compare light and BSDF PDFs in solid angle: `p_omega = p_area * distance² / abs(cos_emitter)`, including the alias-table selection probability. The sampled-light contribution carries a separate `solid_angle_pdf`; its radiance and `inverse_pdf` retain the area formulation used by ReSTIR. The Jacobian uses the world-space triangle cross product, independent of interpolated or normal-mapped shading normals. Straight-through panes and rejected coverage retain the preceding scattering position; delta reflections own subsequent emission.

`cargo test --test emitter_mis -- --ignored --nocapture` executes the production sampling/PDF functions on Vulkan. A 256×256 quadrature over a unit-radiance square emitter gives 0.55412862 against the analytic Lambertian result 0.55412644 at half-width=distance=1; distance=4 gives 0.07347771 against 0.07347764. Scaling both dimensions to 0.1, 4 or 10 preserves radiance, including emitter-selection PMF 0.25. Every sample checks both competing PDFs against an independent CPU formula. Existing glossy glass cases run with and without RR guide code. Bistro realtime/reference captures verify full shader composition; the short reference capture is noisy and is not a convergence measurement. These tests establish the PDF measure correction, not general unbiasedness of the bounded renderer. Backface-emission consistency and shadow transport through glass/coverage remain separate limitations.

Derivation: [PBRT light sampling](https://www.pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Light_Transport_I_Surface_Reflection/Sampling_Light_Sources) and [emissive-hit MIS](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_I_Surface_Reflection/A_Better_Path_Tracer).

The deferred path is still forced, one emissive mesh may have at most 65,535 triangles, and the scene at most 65,535 light sources of every kind together.
